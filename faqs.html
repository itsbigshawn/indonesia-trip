❓ Frequently Asked Questions

🌺 Bali 🛕 Yogyakarta 🏙️ Jakarta ✈️ Flights ❓ FAQs

Do I need a visa?

Yes! It should be very easy to apply via the official e-visa website. Choose the Tourist Visa (Visa on Arrival).

What's the weather like?

I'm not gonna sugarcoat it: it's gonna be hot and humid most of the time. Make sure to bring lots of sunscreen and pack a good hat. If you have a fan, it'll be handy to bring it too.

Rainy season hasn't quite started yet, though when it rains, it usually pours. I'd pack either a small umbrella or a rain jacket just in case.

What should I wear?

What's considered acceptable will vary from region to region. Bali is predominantly Hindu and very tourist-heavy, so you can pretty much wear whatever you want. The only exception is when we're entering temples. You'd be required to cover your shoulders and knees, though most temples will lend you a sarong, which is a long piece of fabric you can wear over shorts to cover your legs.

In other cities like Jogjakarta and Jakarta, people tend to dress more conservatively, so I'd avoid wearing anything too revealing.

Because the weather is mostly hot and humid, linen-based or flowy clothing will help a lot.

How do I pay for stuff?

Since we're sticking to tourist areas, most places accept Visa and Mastercard. Though cashless payments are generally available, carrying a small amount of cash is still a good idea, mostly for tipping and toilets that charge a small fee.

We also have the local payment system called QRIS (QR code payment), which you can use via the Gojek/GoPay app. This is useful if you want to buy from smaller vendors instead of paying with cash, though I'm not sure if you can top up your balance online without a local number.

Is tipping a thing?

Tipping is not mandatory, so it's totally up to your discretion. At restaurants, we usually leave some cash behind as tips after the final payment. For regular meals, I'd usually tip Rp10–20k. For more upscale dining, Rp50k and up is good. Again, it's not mandatory and these are just some guidelines.

Sometimes we also tip drivers and hotel porters, but again, it's not mandatory.

How do we get around?

I'd recommend downloading the Gojek and Grab apps. These are our local Ubers, and we'll be relying on them a lot! You should be able to use your credit cards as payment methods.

Anything else that I need to know or pack?

  • Indonesia is a tropical country, so bring insect repellent since mosquitoes are very common.
  • If you're a light sleeper, I'd recommend bringing earplugs. In Jogja and Jakarta, you'll hear the adzan five times a day from the mosques, and the earliest prayer call might wake you up at 5am.
